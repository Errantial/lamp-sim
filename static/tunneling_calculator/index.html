Quantum tunneling calculator

Explore a particle below a rectangular potential barrier. This tool uses the exponential approximation and keeps very small probabilities in logarithmic form.

Barrier and particle

mₑ is the electron mass.

E and V₀ use the same zero: the incident-region potential.

The copper preset sets d = δ.

Results update as you type. Changing a unit preserves the physical value. Scientific notation such as 9.11e-31 is accepted.

Density inside the barrier

Model and assumptions
κ = √[2m(V0 − E)] / ℏ δ = 1/κ T ≈ exp(−2κd) ρ(x)/ρ(0) = exp(−2κx)
  • A one-dimensional, constant rectangular barrier, with positive mass and width and 0 < E < V0. Zero energy has no incoming flux in this scattering model; values at or above the barrier need a different calculation.
  • Nonrelativistic range: this calculator conservatively requires both E and V0 − E to be below 1% of mc². This is a chosen applicability limit, not a 1% accuracy guarantee for the transmission estimate.
  • δ is the distance over which the amplitude decreases by e−1. The density decreases by e−2 over that distance; its e-folding length is δ/2.
  • The plotted density is divided by its value at the barrier entrance. It is the decaying-component model inside the barrier, not a globally normalized wavefunction.
  • The exponential transmission formula is most useful for strongly attenuating barriers. In particular, the copper preset has κd = 1; its exponential estimate is not an exact finite-barrier transmission calculation.
  • The waiting estimate assumes independent attempts with unchanged probability T. Mean wait = 1/T seconds, using 31,557,600 seconds per year. Applying this single-particle model to a whole person is a formal illustration, not a physical account of macroscopic tunneling.
Reproduce the calculation

Constants: 1 eV = 1.602176634 × 10−19 J; ℏ = (6.62607015 × 10−34 J·s)/(2π); mₑ = 9.1093837139 × 10−31 kg.

Copper electron: m = mₑ, V0 = 4.7 eV, E = 2.7 eV, d = δ. These follow the assignment's simplified common energy reference; a real metal's work function is measured from its Fermi level. Person and wall: m = 60 kg, V0 = 25,000 J, E = 187.5 J, d = 0.03 m.

Hand-calculation checks: copper δ = 0.1380214 nm, ln T = −2 at d = δ, and T ≈ 0.1353353. For the person, ln T = −9.8175015 × 1035 and log₁₀ T = −4.2636867 × 1035. These validate the assigned exponential formula, not omitted boundary-matching factors.

The implementation first converts all inputs to SI units. It computes ln T directly, and obtains log₁₀ T = ln T / ln 10. For one attempt per second, log₁₀(wait in years) = −log₁₀ T − log₁₀(31,557,600).
